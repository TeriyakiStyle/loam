If our vibrant, teeming soil is a party, then sowing a seed in it is like the song and dance.

Our dancer arrives in the form of a soybean. Water wakes it. A root reaches down. A shoot curves toward the light.

At first, the seed carries the food for its own opening steps. Then its leaves unfurl. Sunlight becomes sugar. And the plant has something to share.

Through its roots, some of that sugar enters the soil, feeding life around it. Microbes and fungi help release and carry nutrients. The roots take up what the plant needs to grow.

An exchange takes shape. Sugar from above. Water and nutrients from below. The plant dances between them.

More leaves reach for the light. More roots explore the dark. And around those roots, the gathering grows.

Flowers open. Pods swell. Some of the energy gathered from sunlight becomes food for our table. Some is packed into seeds, ready for another season.

And when the dance is done, we cut the stem and lay it down. The roots remain.

The fallen plant becomes food for the life that helped it grow. As it breaks down, nutrients return to the exchange.
